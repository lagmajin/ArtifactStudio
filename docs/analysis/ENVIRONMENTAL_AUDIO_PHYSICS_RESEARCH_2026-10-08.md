# Environmental Audio Physics Research

**最終更新:** 2026-10-08

## Scope

This note records a focused research pass for the standalone rain, wind, and wave generators in `ArtifactCore`. The cited papers inform model choices; this work does not reproduce their full algorithms or datasets.

## Evidence and implementation

| Source | Reported finding | Model use |
|---|---|---|
| Liu, Cheng, Tong, [Physically-based Statistical Simulation of Rain Sound (SIGGRAPH 2019)](https://haonancheng.cn/attaches/2019%20SIGGRAPH.pdf) | Rain impact is a short transient; bubble oscillation is a distinct event and may be inaudible for solid surfaces. | Existing droplet model keeps randomized short impacts and avoids adding a sustained tonal bubble to every drop. |
| Heutschi et al., [Auralization of Wind Turbine Noise: Propagation Filtering and Vegetation Noise Synthesis (2014)](https://doi.org/10.3813/AAA.918682) | Wind-related sound is shaped by a time-varying wind process and filtered-noise spectra; vegetation contributes a separate, speed-dependent noise source. | Wind now has a slow, asymmetric stochastic gust envelope. The current model remains a generic wind bed and does not claim to synthesize vegetation. |
| Verron and Drettakis, [Procedural audio modeling for particle-based environmental effects (AES 2012)](https://www-sop.inria.fr/reves/Basilic/2012/VD12/) | Their wind model sums several time-varying, band-limited noise atoms whose gain and center frequency respond to wind speed; their rain model separates impact classes by surface. | Wind now emits four independent overlapping band-pass noise tasks. Rain surface modes are tracked separately below. |
| Beacham et al., [Sound generation by water drop impact on surfaces (2020)](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.expthermflusci.2020.110138) | Lab recordings show material-dependent spectra; drops on a deep pool have an initial event near 10 kHz and a later cavity-collapse event near 4 kHz, roughly 50 ms afterward. | Added an opt-in water impact surface with a high-frequency onset and a delayed, damped bubble resonance. Solid remains the default. |
| van den Doel, [Physically based models for liquid sounds (2005)](https://doi.org/10.1145/1101530.1101554) | Liquid sounds can be described as stochastic acoustic emissions from bubbles across streams, rain, rivers, and breaking waves. | Supports event-based liquid sound as a direction; the current wave break remains a simplified broadband event. |
| Guo, [Sound generation in the ocean by breaking surface waves (1987)](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/journal-of-fluid-mechanics/article/abs/sound-generation-in-the-ocean-by-breaking-surface-waves/F183AE007307DFE885A81E45DD1FBCCB) | Spray momentum fluctuations can be an important sound source during wave breaking. | Supports treating a break as a transient/noisy event rather than a continuous pitched tone. |
| Deane and Stokes, [Scale dependence of bubble creation mechanisms in breaking waves (Nature 2002)](https://doi.org/10.1038/nature00967) | Measured bubble populations follow different size regimes: small bubbles from jet/drop impact scale approximately as r^-3/2, while larger bubbles from turbulent fragmentation scale as r^-10/3 around the 1 mm Hinze scale. | Bubble-cloud events now sample those two radius regimes (0.25–1 mm and 1–8 mm) with a continuous population weighting around 1 mm. |
| Wang and Liu, [Example-based synthesis for sound of ocean waves caused by bubble dynamics (2018)](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1002/cav.1857) | Wave audio depends on bubble dynamics and wave attributes, with example clustering used for real-time synthesis. | Follow-up direction: model foam/bubble activity separately from the swell bed; not implemented in this slice. |
| Xue et al., [Improved Water Sound Synthesis using Coupled Bubbles (SIGGRAPH 2023)](https://graphics.stanford.edu/papers/coupledbubbles/) | Their oscillator model uses a dense mass matrix with off-diagonal coupling `sqrt(r_i r_j) / d_ij`; the authors regularize close-bubble coupling to avoid unstable modes. In-phase modes shift lower and create fuller low-frequency content. | Added a bounded two-bubble eigenmode approximation. Optional physical samples now contribute nearest-neighbor position/radius coupling and set the generated resonance radius; without samples, the model uses the statistical fallback. |
| Kim and Lee, [Data-driven and physics-inspired sound synthesis of ocean waves incorporating screened foam particles and projective space (Scientific Reports 2026)](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-026-59852-6) | A low-cost 2D screen-space foam-particle representation controls spatial wave sound; the authors explicitly describe it as a foam-driven approximation rather than full 3D bubble acoustics. | A possible future bridge from Artifact particle/foam state to sound controls; no projective particle data is currently available to this standalone model. |

## Changes in this slice

- Wind gust amplitude follows a bounded, filtered random process with a faster rise and slower fall. Its state uses a fixed PRNG and scalar fields, so `Update` performs no heap allocation.
- Wind now emits four independent, overlapping band-pass noise atoms. Their centers shift with wind speed and their levels share the slow gust envelope, following the broad structure of the AES 2012 wind model. This remains a compact signal approximation rather than the paper's complete time-varying filter implementation.
- Flow synthesis now maps its frequency parameter to a low-pass corner in hertz and computes the one-pole coefficient from sample rate. The previous `frequency / (Q * sampleRate)` coefficient kept low-frequency wind unnaturally muffled. A small unfiltered component preserves broadband air noise.
- Wave cycles vary by a bounded ±8% around the selected period to reduce exact repetition. This is a perceptual approximation, not a claim that real ocean swell follows this distribution.
- Wave breaking now raises a decaying foam-density envelope and emits a separate bubble-cloud task. Each short resonator event maps sampled bubble radius to frequency using the Minnaert inverse-radius relationship (f ≈ 3.28/r in SI units for air bubbles in water); bubble count follows the foam envelope.
- Bubble events now solve the two eigenfrequencies and eigenvectors of a simplified two-bubble mass-coupled oscillator. With physical samples, coupling is estimated from each sample's nearest neighbor as `sqrt(r_i r_j) / max(d_ij, r_i + r_j)`, averaged and capped at 0.45; the geometric-mean sample radius also sets the resonant radii (with bounded ±15% variation). With no samples, bubble sizes follow the Deane–Stokes statistical model and foam density supplies a coupling fallback. This is still a pair approximation, not the paper's full dense oscillator system.
- The standalone test renders coupled and uncoupled bubble-cloud WAVs from identical model input. For the current 8 mm test bubbles, the coupled preview has about 1.91× the 500 Hz one-pole low-pass power of the uncoupled preview. This verifies the expected low-frequency shift, not perceptual realism.
- Rain uses randomized impact grains and event-level stereo spread. Solid impacts remain the default; selecting a water surface changes the initial transient band and adds a damped 3.2–4.8 kHz resonance after 50 ms. This follows the reported two-event pattern, but is not a geometric Helmholtz solver.

## Limits and next work

The wind paper's vegetation source model is not present. Rain does not yet provide calibrated material sound textures for foliage, masonry, or metal. Wave bubble samples are an optional standalone input; they are not yet connected to the app's visual foam output. The existing `LiquidFoamPoint2D` data uses normalized container coordinates and visual sprite size, not calibrated meter positions and physical bubble radii, so it cannot be passed through unchanged. The pair approximation does not solve the paper's full dense oscillator system, and no underwater-to-air propagation is modeled. The standalone WAV checks verify finite, non-silent stereo output, intended low-frequency redistribution, and file structure; they do not evaluate perceptual realism. Listening review remains necessary before treating these models as production quality.
