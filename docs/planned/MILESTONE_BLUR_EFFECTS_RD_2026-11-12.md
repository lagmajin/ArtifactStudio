## 目的

既存のブラー系機能を、見た目・入力・画質・実行コストを同じ基準で比較できる R&D suite として整理する。空間畳み込み、方向場、レンズ／深度、時間履歴、生成的 stylize を再現可能な fixture で評価し、次の投資先を選べるようにする。

## 実装意味論の横断確認

静的に確認できた処理意味論を、capture条件と解釈に反映する。これらはコード経路の確認であり、実出力の数値比較ではない。

| 対象 | 入力色・alpha処理 | 境界／サンプル | captureで分離する点 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Gaussian Blur | CPU/HLSLともRGBをpremultiplyしてalphaと畳み込み、出力時にunpremultiply。 | CPU OpenCVは既定境界（Reflect101）。HLSLは各軸を端pixelへclamp。CPUは6σ程度の奇数kernel、GPUは半径ceil(3σ)を最大64に制限。 | 同一設定でもCPU/GPUの境界・離散kernel・大σ上限が違う。中央impulse parityとborder impulse parityを別記録する。 |
| Blur | CPUは`convertBlurColorSpace()`でsRGB変換を行い、変換関数が各RGBを[0,1]へclampする。既定premultiplied modeではalphaも別にblur。 | CPUは`BORDER_REPLICATE`。GPU samplingも端clamp。CPUのwide blurはhalf-resolution経路がある。`EdgePreserving` branchは各iterationで通常のGaussian後にsigma 0.6倍のGaussianをもう一度適用する。 | HDR/negative fixtureでclippingを明示評価し、linear scene-referred 入力への色変換前提を確認する。Mode=0/1とiterationsを分け、Gaussianとsigmaだけで条件を同一視しない。 |
| Aperture Shape | RGB各planeを個別FFT convolution。alpha planeは入力をそのまま保持。highlightBoostは0.65超成分を追加する。 | `fftConvolve()`は画像寸法のDFTへ直接kernelを配置し、画像側をpadしないため数学的にはcircular convolution。対向端からのwrapをborder impulseで測る。 | alpha edge leakage、PSF energy normalization、custom mask、highlight boost、wraparoundを独立して評価する。 |
| Radial Blur | RGBとalphaを同じsample平均に含める。 | 中心からzoom/spin方向へquality個のsampleを足し、元pixelを含めてquality+1で平均。座標は整数化され、範囲外は端pixelへclamp。 | zoom/spin、中心位置、品質、端部へのsample集中とalpha edgeを評価する。 |
| Anisotropic Flow Blur | BGRA各channelを直接bilinear sampleし、premultiply/unpremultiplyしない。 | structure tensorで局所tangent角を出し、9点を線上sample。境界は座標clamp。`v_len`は計算されるがsamplingには使われない。 | `edgeAdherence`は未使用の`v_len`にしか入らないため現状出力に影響しないように見える。0/1設定をcaptureして確認し、hidden-RGB影響、HDR維持、9-tap固定品質も記録する。横断幅があると仮定して評価しない。 |
| Reaction Diffusion | RGBをGaussian blur後の模様でmixし、alphaは入力値を保持。seedは入力のBGRA grayscaleから作る。 | 初段GaussianとLaplacianはReflect101。模様は低解像度gridで反復し、拡大後に合成する。 | 通常のblur半径順位から分け、alpha edge、HDR/negative、Evolution固定時の決定性、模様の寄与を調べる。 |

初期 spatial benchmark は次の条件に固定する。

- linear float RGBA、256×256 と 1024×1024 の静止 fixture。
- impulse／point light、1px line pair、slanted edge、半透明の赤→透明境界、端に接した高輝度形状を用意する。
- Gaussian、Blur、Aperture Shape は impulse 出力の実測 visible-energy covariance から得る effective σ を主指標に合わせ、95% energy radius・major/minor σ・異方性も併記する。設定値（nominal radius/σ）が同じでも実効量は揃わない。Radial と Anisotropic Flow はこの一致条件に加えて固有の標準設定も記録する。
- CPU/GPU 比較では効果設定、色 descriptor、寸法を一致させ、入力の非破壊を確認する。

- 透明画素 RGB を変えて visible output が変化しないか検査する。HDR は負値と1超値を含み、clipping の有無を記録する。

Capture normalization must obey [`docs/technical/IMAGE_CHANNEL_ORDER_CONTRACT_2026-10-16.md`](../technical/IMAGE_CHANNEL_ORDER_CONTRACT_2026-10-16.md). `ImageF32x4_RGBA` has BGRA physical backing; `setFromRGBA32F` normalizes logical RGBA input to BGRA, while `toCanonicalRGBA32FC4` only swaps channel order. It does not convert alpha association or transfer. Before `.npy` export, require a fully specified source descriptor and use the descriptor-aware conversion path to produce linear scene-referred RGBA straight alpha. Unknown alpha/transfer metadata makes the capture invalid until the effect output contract is established. The external EXR normalizer records an explicit source-contract assertion but does not read Artifact descriptors; this descriptor-aware Artifact capture route remains unimplemented.

**Gaussian capture route decision (source inspection, 2026-11-12):** the existing GPU Render Queue paths do not provide the required evidence in one output. The ordinary beauty route reads float RGBA, applies composition final-image effects, then converts through `ImageF32x4_RGBA::toQImage()` to RGBA8 sRGB straight before encoding; it cannot preserve scene-linear float samples. The multi-channel EXR route reads scene Beauty as float, swizzles BGRA storage to logical R/G/B/A, and writes float EXR, but its documented AOV boundary is before composition final-image effects. Therefore it cannot capture a final-image Gaussian candidate. Additionally, the Render Queue clears to the composition background color, so alpha-edge evaluation requires an explicitly transparent background. The Gaussian GPU wrapper itself has a staging readback, but it returns an internal destination buffer and is not exposed as a general capture endpoint; its wrapper also includes input upload, synchronous flush/wait/readback, so its timing would not be native resident-pass timing.

**Finite-image boundary energy equation study:** `experiments/blur-rd/gaussian_boundary_energy_model.py` compares the CPU source's OpenCV default `REFLECT_101` boundary with the GPU source's separable clamp-sampling equation on opaque 513×513 unit impulses at center, edge midpoint, and corner. The center preserves unit in-frame energy for both models. At sigma 3, clamp/CPU in-frame energy is 3.08× at the edge and 9.50× at the corner; at sigma 16 it is 13.36× and 178.36×. At sigma 64 it reaches 30.44× and 926.55×. These are integrated impulse-energy ratios, not per-pixel brightness ratios or GPU measurements. They show that the current CPU/GPU source equations use materially different finite-image boundary conditions: clamp repeats edge samples while `REFLECT_101` reflects interior samples. This is not by itself a quality verdict; parity requires choosing and documenting the intended boundary contract, then confirming with Artifact captures. Full values, centroids, spread, peaks, source hash, and equation scope are in `experiments/blur-rd/results/gaussian_boundary_energy_model_2026-11-12.json`; `experiments/blur-rd/results/previews/gaussian_boundary_energy_model_2026-11-12.png` shows the corner impulse responses at sigma 3 and 16 on a shared linear scale per sigma.

**Boundary-position sweep extension:** for sigma 3 and 16, the opaque impulse was translated along the center row from the left boundary at distances 0–256 px. The clamp/CPU in-frame energy ratio is strongly non-monotonic near the edge: at sigma 3 it changes from 3.08× at distance 0 to 0.615× at 1 px and 0.722× at 2 px, then reaches 0.995× by 8 px and equality outside the support (16 px). At sigma 16 it changes from 13.36× at 0 px to 0.524× at 1 px, then approaches 0.977× by 32 px and equality outside support (64 px). The equation model therefore predicts both inflation at a border sample and reduced in-frame energy for an adjacent interior sample; a single border-impulse ratio does not describe general content near the edge. These are equation-model ratios only. Positionwise measurements, centroid shifts, full-frame differences, and the log-scaled sweep plot are recorded in the same JSON and `experiments/blur-rd/results/previews/gaussian_boundary_position_sweep_2026-11-12.png`.

**Boundary-policy crossover control:** the same sigma 3/16 impulse positions were recomputed with both border policies in both equation models: OpenCV `REFLECT_101`/`REPLICATE` and shader-equation `reflect101`/`clamp`. With the original CPU `REFLECT_101` vs GPU clamp pair, maximum full-frame RMSE over sampled distances was 3.50e-4 (sigma 3) and 3.54e-4 (sigma 16). When the boundary policies were matched, maximum RMSE fell to 1.25e-10 for replicate/clamp and 5.52e-11 for reflect101 at sigma 3; at sigma 16 it fell to 5.10e-11 and 7.42e-12. This counterfactual control indicates boundary policy dominates the equation-model mismatch at these two sigmas; it does not choose the visually correct policy or prove live CPU/GPU parity. Full crossed-pair RMSE/max-absolute-error measurements and scope are in the `boundary_policy_crossover` section of `gaussian_boundary_energy_model_2026-11-12.json`.

**Sigma support staircase:** `experiments/blur-rd/gaussian_sigma_staircase.py` evaluates all 640 values on the UI's 0.1–64.0, 0.1-step range using float32 source formulas. CPU and GPU finite radii match for 129/640 samples (20.16%); all matches occur through sigma 21.4, after which the shader radius cap of 64 and the CPU radius continuing toward 192 prevent a match. At sigma .1 CPU radius is 0 while shader radius is 1. Across the matched-radius samples, normalized 1D kernel RMSE is at most 3.45e-8 (95th percentile 1.05e-8), supporting that support radius is the dominant discrete difference within this isolated 1D equation comparison. This excludes border extension, separable intermediate rounding, RGBA/alpha handling, and live dispatch; it is not a CPU/GPU image parity finding. The sampled 0.1 grid is not an exhaustive proof over every representable float. Full sigma run intervals, formulas, source/tool hashes, and values are in `experiments/blur-rd/results/gaussian_sigma_staircase_2026-11-12.json`.

**Sigma-step response study:** `experiments/blur-rd/gaussian_sigma_continuity.py` evaluates the full 640-step sigma range on two fixed opaque linear fixtures: a centered unit impulse and a centered 3px line at value4 in 513×513. It derives full-frame response from the separable 1D source kernels and records adjacent-step RMSE, peak changes, spread, route-vs-route error at every sigma, and selected samples around support thresholds/cap. **Support-transition isolation control:** the same continuity model now compares each actual adjacent-sigma output delta with a counterfactual where both endpoints use the larger of their two finite radii. The L2 norm of `actual delta - fixed-radius delta` is a numerical estimate of the contribution from changing support, not a perceptual discontinuity score. There are 192 CPU radius transitions and 63 GPU transitions on the sampled 0.1 grid. For the impulse, support-transition extra-response RMSE p95/max is `7.72e-7` / `1.69e-6` CPU and `6.16e-7` / `8.00e-7` GPU; for the HDR line it is `7.90e-6` / `1.73e-5` CPU and `6.32e-6` / `8.21e-6` GPU. Largest extra-response cases are sigma1.8→1.9 CPU and sigma2.0→2.1 GPU. The max extra-response norm is about 7.2% (CPU) / 4.2% (GPU) of that pair's total step norm for the impulse, and 7.0% / 4.1% for the HDR line; these are vector-norm ratios and do not imply a linear perceptual contribution. The 95th-percentile support extra is below 0.37% of the largest ordinary step with unchanged support in these fixture/route pairs. This control indicates that most measured adjacent change comes from the intended sigma change in the tested fixtures, while finite-support changes add a smaller but nonzero numerical term. Held-radius kernels are a counterfactual and do not represent a product route. Method and all transition rows are recorded in the continuity JSON; live output, visual acceptance, alpha behavior, and performance remain unverified.
